Local know-how

Georgia Tree Problems We Prune For

Trees in Woodstock, Atlanta and North Georgia face conditions most national tree companies never plan for. Here is what we see most across Cherokee, Cobb, Fulton, Forsyth and Gwinnett.

Storm & Ice Limb Failure

Severe thunderstorms, tornado-season winds, tropical remnants and occasional ice storms snap weak-wooded trees like water oak, sweetgum and silver maple. Pruning before the season is the cheapest insurance you can buy.

Saturated Red Clay

Wet clay soil loosens root hold. A heavy, dense crown then acts like a sail. Thinning the crown lowers the load and the chance of uprooting.

Pine Stress & Beetles

Loblolly and other pines weakened by drought or crowding are more vulnerable to bark beetles. We remove dead limbs and flag trees that need a health inspection.

Bradford Pears & Crape Myrtles

Bradford pears split at tight forks and often need structural work or removal. Crape myrtles need proper late-winter pruning, never "crape murder" topping.

Kudzu, Ivy & Wisteria

Invasive vines smother crowns and hide defects. We cut vines at the base and clear them from limbs so you can see the tree's true condition.

Power Lines, HOAs & Ordinances

Some cities, including Atlanta, regulate work on protected trees, and many HOAs set their own rules. We check requirements first and work safely around service drops.

Tree Trimming & Pruning FAQ

Trimming shapes a tree and keeps it clear of roofs, driveways and lines. Pruning removes dead, diseased, weak or crossing branches to improve health and structure. Most jobs combine both.
Late fall through winter (roughly December to February) suits most hardwoods and pines because trees are dormant and easy to inspect. Spring-flowering trees should be pruned right after they bloom. Hazard limbs can be removed any time of year.
Cost depends on tree height, species, access, the amount of work and debris hauling. Most residential jobs fall roughly between $400 and $2,500. We give a written, itemized estimate after a free on-site assessment.
Routine trimming usually does not, but some cities and counties, including Atlanta, regulate work on protected or specimen trees, and HOAs may have their own rules. We check local requirements before we start.
As a general rule, no more than about 25 percent of the live crown in a single season. We do not top trees. Topping causes weak regrowth, decay and higher failure risk.
We trim around your home, driveway and service drops where it is safe. Limbs touching or close to energized lines must be handled by the utility or line-clearance-qualified crews. Call us and we will advise on next steps.
