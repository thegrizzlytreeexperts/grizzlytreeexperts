Best Time to Plant Trees in Georgia

For most trees, plant while the weather is cool and the soil is workable. Roots keep growing in fall and winter even when the top looks dormant, which gives the tree a head start before Georgia’s summer heat.

Container-grown treesOctober to early AprilMost flexible. Summer planting works only with disciplined watering.
Balled-and-burlapped treesNovember to MarchHeavy root balls. Plant while dormant for the lowest stress.
Bare-root seedlingsDecember through March 1Window recommended by the Georgia Forestry Commission.
Evergreens and screensFall or early springFall roots establish before summer. Avoid hard freezes and drought.
Fruit treesLate fall to late winterSpecies and chill-hour needs vary. Ask before buying.

Need a tree in July? It can be done, but expect more frequent watering and a smaller tree. Book fall planting early, since good nursery stock and crew schedules fill up from October onward. Learn more about the state’s seedling program at the Georgia Forestry Commission.

How We Plant a Tree, Step by Step

This is the method we follow on every job. It matches guidance from university extension programs and the Arbor Day Foundation.

Plan and locate

Walk the site, choose the spot, and have utilities marked through 811 before any digging.

Dig wide, not deep

Two to three times wider than the root ball, as deep as the root ball, with sloped sides so roots can spread.

Set and correct

Place the root flare 1 to 2 inches above grade, and loosen or cut circling roots so they grow outward.

Backfill, mulch, water

Native soil, settled with water to remove air pockets, then a mulch donut kept 6 to 12 inches off the trunk.

We avoid heavy fertilizer or amendments in the hole, since roots then stay inside the amended pocket. A newly planted tree needs deep, slow watering throughout its first growing season, and we show you exactly how much and how often.

Utilities, Property Lines, and HOA Rules

Call 811 first

Georgia homeowners and contractors must request utility locates before digging. It is free and prevents damage to gas, water, and communications lines.

Mind overhead lines

Plant tall species away from power lines. A tree that outgrows its spot may later need trimming and pruning or removal.

Check HOA and city rules

Some HOAs approve species, and some cities regulate trees in the public right-of-way. Plant on your side of the property line to avoid neighbor disputes. We do not provide legal advice.

Tree Planting in Georgia: Frequently Asked Questions

What is the best time to plant trees in Georgia?

Fall through early spring is the best window for most container and balled-and-burlapped trees in Georgia, because roots establish while the weather is cool. The Georgia Forestry Commission recommends planting bare-root seedlings from December through March 1. Summer planting is possible with extra watering, but it is harder on young trees.

How much does tree planting service cost in Georgia?

Cost depends on the tree’s size and species, how far it must be delivered, soil and access conditions, and whether mulch, staking, and aftercare are included. In many residential jobs, installation labor is in the same range as the tree itself, so a nursery price alone is only part of the budget. We give a written estimate after reviewing the site.

Do you supply the tree or plant one I already bought?

Both options work. We can help you choose a species that suits your site and plant it, or plant a tree you purchased from a nursery. If you supply the tree, we check root condition and trunk health before planting and tell you if there is a problem.

How deep should a tree be planted?

Dig the hole two to three times wider than the root ball but no deeper than the root ball. The root flare, where the trunk widens into the roots, should sit about 1 to 2 inches above the surrounding soil. Planting too deep is one of the most common causes of slow decline in young trees.

How often should I water a newly planted tree?

Water slowly and deeply at the root zone, typically a few times per week in the first weeks and then weekly through the first growing season, adjusting for rain and Georgia heat. Soil should be moist but never soggy. We give you a watering plan matched to your tree and soil.

Do I need a permit or HOA approval to plant a tree in Georgia?

Georgia has no statewide homeowner permit for planting a tree on your own lot, but some cities and counties regulate planting in the public right-of-way, and many HOAs require approval or limit species. Check with your local planning department and HOA first. Permit requirements are the property owner’s responsibility.

Can you plant trees near power lines or utilities?

Yes, but placement matters. Call 811 before any digging so underground utilities are marked, and choose small-maturing species under overhead lines. Planting a tall shade tree beneath a power line leads to future conflicts with the utility.

How far from my house should I plant a tree?

As a rule of thumb, plant large shade trees at least 20 to 30 feet from the home, medium trees about 15 to 20 feet, and small ornamental trees about 8 to 10 feet. The right distance depends on the species’ mature spread, foundation, driveway, and septic locations.

What trees grow well in Georgia?

Commonly planted choices include willow oak, Nuttall oak, red maple, river birch, eastern redbud, dogwood, crape myrtle, southern magnolia, and loblolly pine. The best choice depends on sun, soil drainage, available space, and whether you want shade, flowers, privacy, or wildlife value.

Should I plant a replacement after tree removal?

Yes, in most cases. Plan the replacement before the old tree comes out, and have the stump ground so the new tree has root space. We handle removal, stump grinding, and replanting as one coordinated job.

Are free tree programs a good alternative?

They can be. The Georgia Forestry Commission sells low-cost seedlings, and Trees Atlanta runs community planting programs. These work well for large lots or volunteer projects. A professional planting service fits better when you want a specific larger tree installed correctly in a specific spot.
