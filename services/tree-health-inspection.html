What it is

A Real Diagnosis, Not a Drive-By Opinion

A tree health inspection is a close, hands-on evaluation of a tree's canopy, trunk, root flare, and the soil around it. A trained arborist looks for the early signals that a tree is stressed, infected, infested, or structurally unsound, often long before the problem is obvious from the driveway.

Georgia trees face a tough combination of heavy red clay, summer drought swings, severe thunderstorms and tropical remnants, and constant development. That mix quietly weakens roots and sets up sudden failures. An inspection tells you which trees are healthy assets and which are liabilities, so you can spend money where it actually protects your home.

Our inspection

What Our Arborists Check on Every Tree

We do not glance at leaves and guess. Each inspection follows the same top-to-bottom checklist so nothing hidden gets missed.

🍃

Canopy & Foliage

Leaf size, color, density, dieback, early fall color, and epicormic sprouts. Thinning or off-color crowns often point to root or vascular trouble.

🪵

Trunk & Bark

Cracks, cankers, seams, wounds, cavities, oozing, and fungal conks. We also look for bark loosening and boring-insect exit holes.

🌿

Branch Structure

Included bark, codominant stems, weak unions, overextended limbs, and hangers. These are the defects that drop limbs on roofs and cars.

🌱

Root Flare & Soil

Girdling roots, buried flare, mounded mulch, compaction, grade changes, trenching damage, and root plate lift after wet storms.

🐛

Pests & Pathogens

Bark beetles, scale, borers, adelgids, root rots, leaf scorch, and cankers. We identify the culprit so treatment is targeted, not guesswork.

📋

Risk Rating & Report

Know the signs

Warning Signs Your Georgia Tree Needs an Inspection

If you notice any of these, do not wait for the next storm to make the decision for you. Keep people and vehicles out from under the tree and call us.

Dead or brittle branches

Leafless limbs in summer, especially in the upper crown, are a classic sign of decline and a falling hazard.

Mushrooms or conks at the base

Fungal fruiting bodies at the root flare or trunk often signal internal decay, even when the canopy still looks green.

New or worsening lean

A tree that shifts after heavy rain, with soil heaving or cracked ground on one side, may be losing root anchorage.

Cracks, splits, or hollow trunks

Vertical seams and open cavities reduce trunk strength. Multi-stem trees with a tight V-shaped union are especially prone to splitting.

Sawdust, pitch tubes, or tiny holes

Fine sawdust-like frass and pitch tubes on pines are common signs of bark beetle attack, which can kill a pine in a few weeks.

Early leaf drop or sparse crown

Sudden yellowing, scorched leaf margins, or a thinning canopy point to drought stress, root loss, or disease.

Sticky residue or black sooty mold

Honeydew and sooty mold on crape myrtles and other trees often indicate scale insects feeding on the sap.

Construction or grade changes nearby

Trenching, paving, pool digs, and fill soil can sever or smother roots. Decline may not show for two to five years.

Local knowledge

Georgia Tree Diseases & Pests We Inspect For

Different parts of Georgia have different problems. Our arborists know what to look for in metro Atlanta suburbs, on the coast, and in the mountains.

Statewide pines

Southern Pine Beetle & Ips Beetles

Loblolly and other pines stressed by drought, lightning, or storm damage are prime targets. Early detection can save neighboring pines.

Clay soils & disturbed sites

Annosus & Armillaria Root Rot

Root diseases rot the anchor system from below. Trees may look fine until a wet, windy day, then topple.

Oaks & maples

Ganoderma & Other Decay Fungi

Butt and root rot silently hollow out the base of hardwoods, including many large water oaks and willow oaks in older neighborhoods.

Stressed oaks

Bacterial Leaf Scorch & Hypoxylon Canker

Leaf scorch and canker fungi are common where drought, compaction, or root injury has weakened oaks and other hardwoods.

Ash trees

Emerald Ash Borer

This invasive borer is confirmed in Georgia and kills untreated ash. Thinning crowns and D-shaped exit holes call for prompt evaluation.

Metro Atlanta landscapes

Crape Myrtle Bark Scale

White, felt-like scale and black sooty mold weaken one of Georgia's most popular landscape trees and spread quickly between plants.

North Georgia mountains

Hemlock Woolly Adelgid

Tiny sap-feeding insects threaten eastern hemlocks around Lookout Mountain, Toccoa, and other higher-elevation properties.

Coastal Georgia

Laurel Wilt & Coastal Stress

Laurel wilt attacks redbay and related trees near Savannah, while salt, flooding, and hurricane winds stress live oaks and pines.

Suburbs statewide

Weak-Wood & Short-Lived Species

Bradford pears, sweetgums, and older tulip poplars and pines often develop split unions or brittle limbs that fail in wind and ice.

Why Georgia is different

Why Georgia Trees Fail, and How Inspection Prevents It

These local conditions drive most of the tree emergencies we respond to. Understanding them is how we spot trouble early.

Red clay & compaction

Georgia's dense clay drains slowly and compacts easily under foot traffic and equipment. Roots struggle for oxygen, stay shallow, and lose grip when soils saturate, which is why healthy-looking trees can uproot after heavy rain.

Storms & saturated ground

Severe thunderstorms, tornado outbreaks, and tropical systems like Hurricane Helene in 2024 have taught Georgia homeowners how quickly wet soil and wind bring down mature trees. Inspections identify high-risk trees before storm season.

Drought stress cycles

Hot, dry stretches followed by soaking rain weaken trees over time and invite bark beetles, canker fungi, and borers. We evaluate stress history, mulch, watering, and soil so trees can recover.

Fast development

In fast-growing areas of Cherokee, Forsyth, Fulton, Cobb, and Gwinnett counties, grading, trenching, and new foundations often damage roots of the mature trees homeowners want to keep.

Older tree canopy

Many Atlanta-area neighborhoods have aging water oaks, willow oaks, tulip poplars, and pines that are reaching the end of their safe lifespan. Inspections show which trees are still worth investing in.

Local rules & permits

Cities such as Atlanta, Roswell, Alpharetta, Marietta, and Savannah regulate the removal of certain trees. An arborist's findings on a dead or hazardous tree can help support your permit request, and we can advise you on the process.
