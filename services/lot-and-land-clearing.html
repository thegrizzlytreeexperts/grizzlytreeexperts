Know before you clear

Georgia-Specific Issues We Plan Around

Land clearing rules and site conditions vary a lot across Georgia — what applies on a Woodstock acre lot may not apply the same way in Savannah or Columbus. Here's what we routinely check before starting a job.

County Land-Disturbance Permits

Many Cherokee, Cobb, Fulton, and Forsyth County jurisdictions require a land-disturbance permit once cleared acreage crosses a set threshold, and some cities layer on their own tree-protection ordinances for heritage or specimen trees. We confirm what applies to your parcel before mobilizing crews.

Red Clay Erosion Risk

Georgia's Piedmont clay soil erodes quickly once stripped bare, especially on slopes. Under the state's Erosion and Sedimentation Act, land-disturbing activity over a certain size requires best management practices — we use mulch groundcover, buffer strips, and silt fencing to keep sites compliant.

Stream & Wetland Buffers

State and local buffer rules typically restrict clearing within a set distance of streams and wetlands. We flag these zones during the site walk so buffer trees stay protected and your project doesn't stall at inspection.

Open Burning Restrictions

Burning debris piles requires a permit from the Georgia Forestry Commission in most counties, and burn bans are common in dry seasons. We default to hauling or mulching debris to avoid the permitting delay entirely.

HOA & Subdivision Covenants

If your lot sits inside an HOA, architectural review boards often require sign-off before clearing — particularly for tree removal along shared property lines or common-area buffers. We can work directly with your HOA's documentation.

Wildfire & Storm Readiness

In North Georgia's wildland-urban interface areas, clearing brush and defensible space around structures also reduces wildfire risk, while coastal and South Georgia properties benefit from clearing weak trees ahead of hurricane season. See our storm prep services for ongoing protection.
